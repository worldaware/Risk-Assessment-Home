## Page title (browser tab and SEO title)

Home walk-through | World Aware

## Headline

A walk-through of your home, with a plan you can follow

## Intro

The risk check on your phone tells you which hazards to worry about. It can't see your house. I come to the property, look at what is actually there, and leave you a written plan in the order to do it.

## What a walk-through includes

**The property.** I walk the house and the lot with you. I look at the five feet around the house, the roof and vents, where the water heater and shutoffs are, and what would burn, flood or break first.

**The ways out.** We go over the routes you would use to leave, and the backup if the main road is closed. We talk about where everyone meets and who picks up whom.

**Your numbers, checked against what is there.** If you ran the risk check, I go through your scores with you. Some go up once I see the property and some go down.

**A written plan, in order.** You get a short plan that says what to do this week, this month and before your insurance renews, with the hazard-specific work after that. It lists the free programs you qualify for, like a Wildfire Partners assessment or the City of Boulder's Wildfire Resilience Assistance Program, so you don't pay for something the county or city will help with.

## Who it is for

Households and HOA boards in Boulder County. Renters are welcome; most of the plan is about people, papers and getting out, and none of that depends on owning the house.

## What to have ready

- Your homeowners or renters insurance declarations page
- A list of everyone in the house, including pets, and anyone who needs medication, power for a medical device, or help moving
- Your risk check PDF if you ran one (open it on your phone or print it)
- Access to the attic, crawlspace or garage if you have them

## HOA workshop variant

For a second section on the same page, or a separate page at beworldaware.com/hoa.

### Headline

A preparedness workshop for your HOA

### Body

I run the World Aware risk check live with your neighbors in the room. Everyone scores the hazards for their own home, and we compare notes on what the neighborhood shares: the roads out, the people who will need a hand, and who has a generator or a truck.

The board gets a written summary afterward with the hazards the neighborhood ranked highest, the steps households can take on their own, and the few that work better done together, like clearing common areas or agreeing on a meeting point.
